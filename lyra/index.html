Lyra

Put your medicines on it. Lyra shows what to take, knows what you took, and tells your family only when something is wrong.

6 weighing bays 4.2″ e-ink, 400×300 one knob no app any packaging

This replaces my earlier meditation draft: I misread “medication” as “meditation”. Everything below is for the medication station.

01 · The problem

Four questions people on daily medicines ask every day

Lyra is for anyone taking three or more medicines a day, and it is built first for older adults living alone. The WHO's 2003 report on long-term therapies estimates that about half of patients in developed countries don't take their medicines as prescribed. Lyra removes four everyday reasons for that.

Did I already take it?

Doubt leads to double doses or skipped doses. Lyra's home screen always answers it, and it blocks a second dose out loud.

What do I take now?

The cups for this time glow. The screen says how many pills. There is nothing to read from a list.

When does this run out?

Lyra counts every pill that leaves the box and tells the family seven days before it runs out.

Is Mum okay today?

Family get one message when something is wrong, not a daily chore of calling to ask.

Existing tools each miss something. Phone apps need the phone, which older people often don't carry around the house. Plastic pill organisers don't remind or record anything. Automatic dispensers need every pill reloaded into their own cartridges. Lyra works with the packs people already have, in any country: bottles, blister boxes and weekly organisers.

03 · A day with Lyra

The person never presses anything to be reminded or to record a dose

  1. 07:12
    First movement seen. The radar notes the first time someone passes today. If no one has been seen by the family's chosen time (say 10:00), they get a message.
  2. 08:00
    Morning cups glow; the screen says “Morning · take 2 pills”. No sound yet. Most people take their pills within 15 minutes and never hear Lyra.
  3. 08:12
    They lift box 2, take one pill and put it back. The weight drops by one pill (620 mg), so Lyra records “Metformin, 1 pill, 08:12” and ticks Morning. No button is needed.
  4. 19:15
    They're home and the evening dose is 15 minutes late. Lyra chimes. At +30 minutes it says “Time for your evening pills” in their language. If nobody is home, it waits quietly and reminds them when they walk back in.
  5. 21:00
    Unsure, they open box 2 again. Lyra says “Already taken at 19:12. Please put it back.” If the extra pill goes back in the box within 10 minutes, nothing else happens. If not, the family is told.
  6. 22:05
    Night pill: Levothyroxine, 100 mg. One pill is too light to count reliably by weight, so the screen asks “Did you take it?” and one press answers it.
  7. 01:00
    The night dose's window closes. Any dose not taken by its window's end (01:00 for the night dose) is marked missed. The family gets one message with context: “home and reminded 3 times” or “away all evening”.
04 · Screens

One question per screen, readable from across the kitchen

The screens use pictograms for times of day, so they work before any translation. State is shown by shape, not colour: a filled tick means taken, an empty ring means still to take, a cross means missed. The largest type is 40 px or more. The mockups use Atkinson Hyperlegible, a typeface designed for low vision.

05 · The logic

Rules the firmware follows, with the numbers in the code

Only settled weights count

A reading is used only after the weight has held steady for one second: 10 samples at 10 Hz that agree within 6σ. The detector compares the settled weight before and after, so hands, knocks and a box being carried around never count as a dose. In the test, 20 knocks on the tray produced 0 dose events.

Counting pills is a probability

For each possible count (0, 1, 2, …) Lyra computes the chance that the change came from that many pills. It combines load-cell noise, put-down error and 4% pill-to-pill spread. The count is recorded silently only at ≥90% confidence. Otherwise it asks once.

Dose windows

A dose may be taken from 60 minutes early. It is on time within ±60 minutes. It can still be taken until min(180 min, half the gap to the next dose), the usual “skip it if it's almost time for the next one” rule. A pharmacist can override that window for each medicine.

Extra pills

If pills come out with no dose open, Lyra says why: already taken (with the time) or not yet (with the opening time). There is a 10-minute put-back window, and the family is told only if the pill isn't returned.

Reminder ladder

At the due time the cups glow, silently. At +15 minutes it chimes, and at +30 minutes it speaks, up to three more times 10 minutes apart. It escalates only while someone is near Lyra. When they come back after being out, it reminds them at once.

Personal timing

After 7 days, if someone reliably takes a dose at the same time (spread ≤ 60 minutes), the reminder moves to their time, between 30 minutes earlier and 45 minutes later. Lyra stays quiet when they are on track.

Pill weight

Setup puts 10 pills on the empty bay: one reading gives the pill weight to about ±3 mg. If setup skips that step, Lyra learns the weight from the first doses. Learning is accurate only for pills of 250 mg or more: for light pills the lightest readings fall under the detection threshold, which biases the estimate upward. So the setup page requires the 10-pill step for anything lighter.

Honest records

Every dose stores how it was confirmed: weighed, learning (weight not yet known) or user-confirmed (knob press). Reports keep these apart, so nobody mistakes a button press for a weighed count.

The one number hardware must hit: put-down repeatability of about 20 mg. At 30 mg, the counting line moves from 150 mg to 250 mg and Lyra asks more often. The self-centring cup and the 500 g cell exist to hit 20 mg, and the first bench test (EVT) measures it.

What Lyra does not claim

Lyra knows pills left the box, not that they were swallowed. Every pill monitor has this limit, including the electronic caps used in clinical trials. Routine drift is “a change worth a phone call”, not a diagnosis. Lyra makes no dosing decisions: the schedule comes from the prescription, entered by a person.

08 · Family, pharmacy, home

No app for the person; ordinary messages for everyone else

Lyra sends each alert as a small JSON event; that part is in the firmware. Turning events into WhatsApp, SMS or e-mail, the setup page and the doctor PDF belong to the cloud service, which is designed but not built yet. The messages below are examples of its output.

WhatsApp · Lyra for Maa · 22:00Maa missed her evening Metformin. She was home and was reminded 3 times.
SMS · Lyra for Maa · 09:10Amlodipine runs out on Tue 8 Oct (6 left). Reply 1 to send the refill to Apollo Pharmacy.
WhatsApp · Sunday summary26 of 28 doses taken, 24 on time. Saturday evenings were missed twice. Morning pills moved from about 08:10 to 09:30 this week.

Setup: a web page, not an app

Lyra shows a QR code. The caregiver or pharmacist opens it in any phone browser, joins the home Wi-Fi, scans each pack's barcode with the browser camera (GS1 DataMatrix or QR), sets the times and puts 10 pills on the bay.

Family replies show on Lyra

A short WhatsApp or SMS reply to Lyra's number appears after the next dose. Scripts the device font can't draw are pre-rendered in the cloud.

Doctor report

A monthly PDF lists taking, timing and correct-day percentages for each medicine, with how each dose was confirmed.

Agvika OS, later

Lyra already publishes presence and dose events as small JSON messages. Agvika can subscribe to the same stream to learn daily routines across the home. Nothing in Lyra has to change.
